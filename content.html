Real Numbers — Euclid's Division Lemma

📘 Mathematics 🕑 8 min read 🗓️ Updated Oct 2026

Save this for offline reading — the download is watermarked with its source link.

Introduction

Euclid's Division Lemma is a fundamental result used to find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two positive integers. It states that for any two positive integers a and b, there exist unique integers q and r such that:

a = bq + r,   where 0 ≤ r < b

Here, a is the dividend, b is the divisor, q is the quotient and r is the remainder.

Euclid's Division Algorithm

This lemma forms the basis of an algorithm to compute the HCF of two positive integers, applied repeatedly until the remainder becomes zero:

  1. To find HCF of c and d, where c > d, apply the lemma to get c = dq + r.
  2. If r = 0, d is the HCF of c and d.
  3. If r ≠ 0, apply the lemma to d and r, and repeat.
Solved Example 1.

Find the HCF of 420 and 130 using Euclid's division algorithm.

420 = 130 × 3 + 30
130 = 30 × 4 + 10
30 = 10 × 3 + 0

Since the remainder is now 0, the HCF of 420 and 130 is 10.

Practice Question.

Use Euclid's Division Algorithm to find the HCF of 196 and 38220.

38220 = 196 × 195 + 0

Since the remainder is 0 on the first step, the HCF is 196.

Key Points to Remember

  • Euclid's Division Lemma applies only to positive integers.
  • The remainder r always satisfies 0 ≤ r < b.
  • The algorithm terminates because the remainder strictly decreases at each step.
